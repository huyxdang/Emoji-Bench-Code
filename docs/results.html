Emoji-Bench · Results Summary

When a model's context is 🫧 quietly wrong, does it notice?

A benchmark for context-pollution recovery: models are handed a step-by-step derivation that already contains one planted error, told only to continue, and scored on whether they end up correct anyway. It measures the model-intrinsic recovery floor with no external feedback — the quantity that bounds agent reliability between verifier checkpoints.

14 models 2 prompt levels · L0 / L1 100 procedurally-generated items 4 difficulty tiers contamination-proof by construction

The task

One planted error, then “Please continue.”

Each item is a 3-message conversation in a freshly generated finite algebra over emoji symbols. The assistant turn is pre-filled with a partial derivation whose middle step is deliberately wrong — and every wrong branch is guaranteed to reach a different final answer than the truth, so the final symbol is diagnostic of whether the model trusted the bad state or repaired it.

Result 1 · Unprompted recovery (L0)

Left alone, models span the entire range

Final-answer correctness when the follow-up is a bare “Please continue.” Bars show 95% Wilson intervals; the dashed line is the random-guess baseline (~24%, since items have 3–6 symbols). The spread is enormous — from at-chance to near-perfect — and, as the next section shows, a low bar here does not mean the same thing for every model.

Result 2 · The hint (L0 → L1)

A five-word nudge closes most of the gap

Adding “Double-check any step you're unsure about” lifts nearly every frontier model to 91–100%. The capability to recover is already present; at L0 it simply isn't deployed spontaneously. This propensity-vs-capability gap is the central finding. Two families are the exception — Mistral Large and Magistral barely move, hinting at a different, capability-bound story (see Result 4).

Result 3 · How they recover

Same score, different behavior

Parsing each continuation into an expression tree and comparing its first step against the clean, corrupted, and pre-error states reveals how a model responds — not just whether it lands right. Three signatures emerge at L0: GPT-5.4/5.5 re-derive the bad step corrected in place; Claude models continue from a silently repaired state; weaker models trust the corruption and cascade. Full restarts are rare everywhere.

L0 condition, share of 100 episodes. Blue + green = genuine recovery; red = blind cascade.

Recovery is real, not luck

Across all 2,544 episodes, only ~1.8% land on the right answer via a mathematically invalid derivation. The step-level validator catches these compensating-error cases the final-answer metric would miss.

The hint changes engagement, not strategy

At L1 the recovery isn't from models starting over — restarts stay rare. The nudge flips blind cascades into in-place corrections and corrected-continues.

Result 4 · The survival metric

Separating “can't” from “won't check”

A low L0 score is ambiguous: is the model bad at the task, or does it blindly trust bad context? The survival rate resolves it — accuracy on the corrupted item divided by accuracy on the identical clean item (same seeds, error removed). Three models have complete clean-control runs so far, and they already make the point sharply:

Magistral solves 84% clean, survives only 21% of planted errors

Its dismal headline is not task inability — it can do these problems. It is near-total trust in the corrupted context. The survival metric turns an ambiguous 18% into a specific diagnosis. (GPT-5.4-mini/nano, by contrast, keep ~82% of their competence under corruption.)

Interpretation

What the numbers say

Frontier reasoning models mostly do self-correct

GPT-5.4/5.5 catch and fix the planted error unprompted in ~96% of episodes. The pessimistic “LLMs can't self-correct” reading is too strong for this class.

…but many capable models won't, unless asked

Opus 4.7/4.8 and Gemini 3.1 Pro sit near or below chance at L0 yet jump to 91–100% at L1. The failure is dispositional, not a capability ceiling.

Weaker models are absorbing, not recovering

Mistral/Magistral propagate the corrupted state to the end. In an agent loop between verifier checkpoints, that is the dangerous mode: errors compound instead of self-limiting.

The construct is context-trust, not arithmetic

Because ground truth is verifiable and the error is off-policy (foreign to the model), this cleanly measures whether a model audits its context or takes it as given — the quantity that matters for memory poisoning, tool-output errors, and multi-agent handoffs.
